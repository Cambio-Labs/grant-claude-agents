# Organizational Voice Profile — Cambio Labs

Last updated: 2026-08-12

This is Cambio Labs' real voice profile, not a template. It states the voice as **rules**.
`voice-library/` holds the **examples**, and `voice-library/audit/voice-audit-guide.md` is the org's
own voice guide, built from a real audit of outbound copy — **that file is authoritative and this one
has been updated to match it.** Where anything below conflicts with the audit guide, the guide wins.

## Scope — what this file governs

Read this alongside the profile card the voice-library router selects. They cover different things,
and mixing them up produces either bland prose or off-register prose:

| This file governs — always | The voice-library profile card governs |
|---|---|
| Preferred terminology (absolute — no example overrides it) | Rhythm and sentence-length variation |
| Inclusive language and no-othering | How a paragraph opens and lands |
| Evidence-based over aspirational posture | Structural and rhetorical moves |
| Not pleading; letting evidence carry the weight | Register and intensity |

**The sentence-length, active-voice, and emotional-language settings below are written for funder-facing
prose.** They are binding for grant proposals, LOIs, and funder questionnaires — `grant-voice` applies
them in full. For everything else (`/cambio-voice`: social, speeches, pitches, outreach), the matched
profile card governs rhythm and intensity, and these settings are a default to depart from
deliberately rather than a ceiling. The terminology table stays absolute in every case.

## Reading level — the axis that actually decides which vocabulary to use

This is the single most load-bearing distinction in this file, sourced directly from the audit
guide's AI rules. **Which words to use depends on who's reading, not what kind of document it is:**

| Audience | Reading level | Vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| Community-facing — participants, prospective participants, general public, social media, signup forms, Journey UI | **6th–8th grade.** Short sentences, plain verbs, no jargon. | Core Terminology table below. Never the Funder-Facing Word Bank — "addressing economic exclusion" is wrong on a signup form; say "free" and "for NYCHA residents." |
| Funder-facing — grant proposals, LOIs, sponsor decks, institutional funder emails, LinkedIn, partnership announcements | Elevated register permitted. | Core Terminology table **plus** the Funder-Facing Word Bank below. |

If you're unsure which register applies, ask who reads this specific piece — not what channel it's
on. A LinkedIn post to funders is funder-facing; a LinkedIn post announcing a resident pitch event
(see the audit's rows 21–22) speaks to both at once and should lead with whichever the specific post
is actually for.

## Formality

professional-warm

---

## Sentence Length

15–20 words average; mix simple and complex sentences.
Break any sentence longer than 30 words into two.

---

## Active Voice

80% preferred — rewrite passive constructions when natural to do so.

---

## Vocabulary

- Avoid jargon without explanation and corporate-speak
- Emotional language: no more than one emotionally weighted adjective or phrase per paragraph; avoid words like "devastating," "crisis," "dire," "urgent plea" — the evidence speaks; the language does not need to plead
- Power words (use sparingly — no more than once per section): transform, empower, sustainable, community-driven, community-led, resilient
- **Never substitute for a real update:** "thrilled," "excited to share," "amazing." The audit found
  these are the sound of a brand managing itself rather than saying what happened — get specific
  instead (name the partner, the number, the outcome). Confirmed independently by two sources: this
  file's own emotional-language rule above, and the audit guide's Rule 05 / AI NEVER rule 2.

---

## Tone

- Collaborative, not hierarchical
- Evidence-based, not aspirational
- Humble but confident
- Inclusive language — no othering language
- **Name gaps plainly; don't inflate confidence to hide them.** If there's an unmet need — a funding
  gap, a program limitation — state it directly rather than dressing it up as momentum. This is the
  same discipline `grant-voice` already protects via Evidence Gap Notices, and the audit guide's
  Rule 03 extends it to every other channel. Real example already in Cambio's own materials: "Funding
  to support ongoing programs ($50K per 6-month cohort): Seeking."
- **Under pressure, get more specific, not less.** When a topic is contested or high-stakes, the
  instinct to retreat into safe generalities is exactly backwards — Cambio's most authentic writing
  names the actual stakes. (Audit guide Rule 05.)

## Preferred Terminology

Two tables. Which one applies is governed by the **Reading level** section above — audience, not
document type, decides.

### Core Terminology — always, every audience

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| participants | clients, recipients |
| community members | target population, the underserved |
| South Bronx | "underserved area", "low-income neighborhood" as primary label |
| community-led | top-down, charity model |

### Funder-Facing Word Bank — funder-facing copy only, never community-facing

Sourced from `voice-library/audit/voice-audit-guide.md` Rule 08. This vocabulary survives compression
— it appears nearly word-for-word across Cambio's funder emails and the Founder's bio, written months
apart, which is why it's treated as core ethos rather than one document's phrasing.

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| underestimated (communities, New Yorkers) | underserved, disadvantaged, at-risk |
| systemic barriers | challenges, obstacles |
| economic exclusion | poverty, low income |
| digital inequity | the digital divide, lack of tech access |
| environmental injustice | environmental issues |
| collective ownership / worker cooperatives | jobs, employment |
| agents of change | beneficiaries, recipients |

**Reconciling with Core Terminology:** "participants" (Core) and "agents of change" (Funder-Facing)
are not competing choices for the same slot. "Participants" names people currently enrolled in a
program, often with a count ("270 participants"). "Agents of change" is aspirational mission-framing
language ("equipping underestimated New Yorkers to become agents of change") — it describes a role,
not enrollment status. Funder-facing copy can and does use both in the same piece; community-facing
copy uses only "participants," never "agents of change" or "beneficiaries."
