# Anew Final Pitch — delivered version

## What this source is

The delivered version of Cambio Labs' Anew accelerator final pitch. Pairs directly with
[`pitch-script-v3.md`](pitch-script-v3.md) — that file is the written script, this is how it actually
sounded out loud, same pitch, same night. **The content is close to identical** (same Tasha story,
same three-pillar structure, same ask) — so this file doesn't re-excerpt what's already captured
there. Its value is the *delta*: the small, consistent things a live speaker adds that a written
script doesn't, which is the most useful thing in the library for spoken-vs-written calibration.

## What changed between script and delivery

- **Acronyms get glossed live that the script left bare.** Script: "a resident of NYCHA, or public
  housing." Delivered: "a resident of NYCHA, New York City's public housing system." The speaker adds
  a definitional clause for an audience that may not already know the acronym — the script assumes
  more shared context than the room actually has.
- **Numbered structure gets made explicit out loud.** The written script's three scaling tactics have
  no numbering on the page. Delivered, they become "Growth Strategy 1: Expand Journey and Our Course
  Ecosystem," "Growth Strategy 2: Activate Community Learning Spaces," "Growth Strategy 3: Use
  Technology and AI to Scale Access" — explicit signposting that helps a listener (who can't scan
  ahead the way a reader can) track where they are in a multi-part argument.
- **"Tech" becomes "technology."** Consistently, across the piece — the spoken register avoids the
  script's clipped abbreviation. Minor, but consistent enough to be a real preference, not noise.
- **"Imagine a future where..." becomes three separate "Imagine..." sentences.** The script chains
  the vision into one sentence with line breaks; delivered, each image gets its own full sentence and
  its own beat — slower, more deliberate pacing live than the page suggests.
- **The close adds one clause of connective explanation the script doesn't need.** Script: "we hope to
  learn what kind of infrastructure we need for global scale, and how to overcome challenges..."
  Delivered: "...and how we can overcome barriers to bringing education to millions of disconnected
  people. That could include leveraging mesh internet networks..." — "that could include" is added
  live to bridge from the abstract goal to the concrete list, something the page's formatting (a
  colon plus a list) handled for a reader but a listener needs said aloud.

**Takeaway for drafting spoken pieces from a written base:** when adapting written copy to be spoken,
expect to (1) define any acronym or org-specific term the way this delivery does, (2) make implicit
structure explicit with spoken signposting ("first... second... third," not just paragraph breaks),
and (3) add the small connective phrases a page's formatting was doing silently.

## Characteristic excerpts — passages distinct enough from the script to be worth keeping

**Acronym glossed live**

> Tasha lives in Harlem and is a resident of NYCHA, New York City's public housing system.

**Explicit numbered signposting, absent from the written script**

> To scale from serving approximately 1,000 learners historically to 15,000 learners in the next
> year, we will implement several growth strategies.
>
> Growth Strategy 1: Expand Journey and Our Course Ecosystem
>
> We will continue partnering with course creators who want to digitize and gamify their own courses
> on Journey, while expanding our catalog of available courses.

**Vision images given room to breathe, one per sentence**

> Imagine a future where Journey is an app reaching hundreds of thousands of people on their phones.
> Imagine Journey in public libraries and embedded into our cities' public infrastructure. Imagine
> Cambio Spaces operating as open-learning hubs in cities around the world, powered by community
> members and volunteers.

**The close, live version — same content as the script, same effect**

> Beyond funding, we are also looking for advisers, program partners, technologists, and volunteers
> who can support our vision of delivering universal access to education and economic opportunity
> through the power of technology.
>
> Join our revolution of empathy and our simple belief that all humans deserve a chance at meaningful
> learning and economic opportunity.
>
> Thank you.
