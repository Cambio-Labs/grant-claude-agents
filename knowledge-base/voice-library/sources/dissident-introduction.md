# DISSIDENT (Introduction) — voice reference

## What this source is

This is the front matter to *Dissident*, subtitled "The Unknown Story of a Revolutionary" — a hip hop concept album by Sebastián Martín now being devised as a staged musical about an undocumented immigrant drawn into street violence, grassroots organizing, state repression, and empire. The introduction is not the script itself; it is a manifesto-plus-user-manual addressed to future producers, directors, and artists, granting them near-total license to adapt the work so long as its anti-authoritarian, anti-imperialist, humanist core survives. It moves through several registers in a few pages: logline, permission grant, values declaration, production advice for broke creatives, cast breakdown, safety warning, and a formal legal disclaimer, closing on an epigraph from Frantz Fanon. The stance is unhedged and openly political — it names capitalism, imperialism, and authoritarianism as antagonists rather than describing "systemic barriers."

## How the voice works

- **Lists of three to six do the heavy lifting.** Almost every important noun arrives as a series, usually escalating from concrete to abstract or from personal to geopolitical: "street violence, grassroots activism, state repression, and international systems of empire"; "survival, love, grief, dissent, solidarity, and revolutionary liberation." Generic nonprofit prose picks one safe abstraction; this voice stacks the whole range and lets accumulation create weight.
- **Paired opposites are a structural habit, not decoration.** "light and dark, good and bad, conformist collaborator and rebellious conspirator"; "In some places, free speech... may be a well-protected constitutional right; in other places..." The antithesis usually lands the paragraph.
- **Second person, imperative mood, permission granted outright.** It talks *to* a reader it treats as a co-conspirator: "you are hereby granted permission to make it your own"; "Honor the talents, stories, and causes of your local community"; "Take on the mindset of a grassroots community organizer." The subject is "we" (collective/institutional), never "I", even though the work is solo-authored.
- **Abstraction is immediately followed by operational specifics in parentheses.** "be scrappy (i.e., start with public readings, low-cost basic staging, and prioritize content delivery over production value)"; "nontraditional and publicly-accessible performance spaces (i.e., small stages and theatres, community centers, auditoriums, libraries, in public housing, and on the streets)." The visionary register never floats free of logistics — the parenthetical is where the organizer shows up.
- **Rhetorical questions arrive as cascades.** A single sentence will run fifteen items long and end in a question mark, functioning as a brainstorm handed to the reader rather than a query the writer answers.
- **Concede, then hold the line.** "That being said," and "we realize that" precede the counter-pressure (people can’t afford tickets; dissent gets people jailed), and the sentence after the concession is always the harder ask, not a retreat.
- **Register shifts are abrupt and unapologized-for.** Manifesto bullets sit a page from a First Amendment disclaimer; bolded headers do the transitional work that connective prose would do elsewhere. Sentences get longest and most semicolon-chained exactly where the stakes are highest, then snap to a five-word declarative: "*Dissident* is a work of fiction."
- **Characters and roles are named by epithet-appositive.** Name, comma, function: "*CHASKA,* community leader and maternal figure, spearheading an underground resistance movement." No backstory, no adjective-hedging — the title tells you the moral position.

## Characteristic excerpts

**Opening logline — abstraction stacked, then flipped into paradox**

> *Dissident* follows the story of an undocumented immigrant navigating life in the United States while becoming entangled in street violence, grassroots activism, state repression, and international systems of empire. The work was written to reflect many of the experiences, ethical grapplings, and political discourse surrounding immigration, interventionism, and the struggles of oppressed people globally. Through music, skits, and theatrical narrative, the original version of *Dissident* explores themes of survival, love, grief, dissent, solidarity, and revolutionary liberation, while critiquing corrosive aspects of American capitalism, imperialism, and authoritarianism. *Dissident* explores the duality and contradiction of light and dark, good and bad, conformist collaborator and rebellious conspirator within every human and every humanist revolution.
>
> *Dissident* was originally created as a hip hop concept album, with radio-play inspired skits recorded between songs. It is now being devised and produced as a staged musical.

**Direct address — handing the reader authority, then one short condition**

> *Dissident* is meant to be a flexible work. Adaptable by design, the spirit of Project Dissident is to be a decentralized platform and vehicle for the voices, needs, struggles, narratives, and revolutionary causes of other artists and communities across the world.
>
> As someone interested in bringing this work to life, you are hereby granted permission to make it your own: subvert, adapt, and change as much or as little of it as you see fit. From the names and story arcs of characters, to dramatized skits or scenes, to lyrics, and even the genre of music - you have carte blanche to make any edits or changes. The original version of *Dissident* was created from the perspective of a Latin American protagonist battling against systems of oppression within the United States, but we are extremely interested to see how this work is adapted by other nations, cultures, and activists worldwide.
>
> We only ask that the original spirit and core intention of Project Dissident remain preserved.

**Values as declarative manifesto — bolded term, colon, unhedged definition**

> *Dissident* is meant to be:
>
> - **Anti-authoritarian:** against the abuse of excessive and corrupted government power
> - **Representative** of marginalized and often unheard voices – oppressed people that survive, despite the system, at the margins of society and power
> - An act of **free expression** of untold truths, free speech, and dissent against unjust government policies, agencies, or oppressive institutions and conditions generally
> - **Anti-imperialist**: criticizing and exposing the reality and harm of colonialism, unjust war and interventionism, invasion and regime change, coups, and empire building
> - **Humanist:** in that all human lives are worth fighting for and all human stories are worth telling; in that all of our individual struggles are connected through our common humanity
> - **Empowering:** so that this work of art serves a political purpose in provoking critical thought around oppressive social conditions, and in developing political consciousness
> - **A call to action** for others to join movements, lend talents, build grassroots people power, produce their own dissident art, and take organized action against oppression

**Menu framing — refusing to prescribe a single path**

> Here are a few suggestions of how you might access or interpret *Dissident* for the purpose of realizing your own production. Ultimately, we leave it up to you: “choose your own adventure” style. Feel free to do things in any order you wish, or to take on a completely different approach.
>
> ➡ Option A: Start with the Music
>
> *Dissident* was originally recorded as a 14-track hip hop concept album, complete with verses from rappers and dramatized skits performed by voice actors.
>
> Listen to the musical album in its entirety here,
>
> Interpret and imagine how you might devise, stage, or produce it yourself.
>
> ➡ Option B: Start with the Words
>
> Read the “Dissident Script” in the following pages, complete with lyrics and stage directions. This represents the original vision of *Dissident*, as created by writer Sebastián Martín.
>
> You can stage your version of *Dissident*, as it is written. Or you can interpret the text and imagine how you might rewrite it, compose and arrange your own music to it, or create your own interpretation of any characters, scenes, and stage directions.

**Epithet-appositive character sketches**

> ***DISSIDENT,*** our main protagonist, an undocumented immigrant and nascent revolutionary
>
> ***SANTOS,*** the nemesis of our tale, a mysterious and dark powerbroker that pulls strings to track, manipulate, and exploit DISSIDENT
>
> ***SORAYA,*** the beloved of DISSIDENT, the visionary and moral core of the humanist revolution
>
> ***CHASKA,*** community leader and maternal figure, spearheading an underground resistance movement
>
> ***DON MANUEL,*** a nefarious kingpin and gangster that profits from violence and crime

**Naming the antagonist before making the ask**

> In a world where the theatre industry has become big business, and producers often make safe choices to avoid ruffling corporate and government feathers – we encourage you to be bold in making your directorial and production choices for *Dissident*, and to appeal to the "uninitiated": artists and audience members - especially from low income and oppressed communities - that have not traditionally been included in theatre or activism.

**Concession, then the harder ask — with the logistics in parentheses**

> We hope that *Dissident* productions can become a means of income and professional development for the creatives and producers that take it on. That being said, many everyday working people can’t afford to buy tickets to theatre shows. So in the interest of creating widespread access to this work, we encourage you to always include a tier of low-cost or free tickets to *Dissident,* that can be accessed by all people.
>
> We encourage productions to be scrappy (i.e., start with public readings, low-cost basic staging, and prioritize content delivery over production value), and to experiment with alternative funding and production models (i.e., leveraging sponsorship and crowdfunding, mutual aid, and volunteer efforts) to make your production happen. We encourage staging this work in nontraditional and publicly-accessible performance spaces (i.e., small stages and theatres, community centers, auditoriums, libraries, in public housing, and on the streets).

**Imperative stacking — short commands in a row**

> We encourage you to involve underrepresented local artists and community members in your production (especially if you are adapting this work). Honor the talents, stories, and causes of your local community. Make your production authentic and reflective of your unique local context. Take on the mindset of a grassroots community organizer that seeks to understand the needs and perspectives of their neglected community members as you execute your production. Create opportunities for emerging local artists, and personalize and adapt this work to match your lived reality. *Dissident* is meant to be an ever-evolving vehicle for diverse voices and causes across the world.

**The question cascade — a brainstorm disguised as a sentence**

> In the spirit of broader movement-making, we encourage you to organize “Dissident Events” that rally other activists, political groups, and community based organizations around your local production. How might you incorporate workshops, performances from other local artists, fundraisers, free social services, resource tables, community listening sessions, participatory design challenges, political and activist trainings, voter registration initiatives, skills sharing sessions, mutual aid initiatives, volunteer recruitment platforms, promotions of social causes, activist campaign speeches, calls to action, and any other content that may benefit the community? How might your production of *Dissident* function as an anchor and community-building space that empowers immigrant, low-income, and oppressed people?

**Turn from vision to peril — semicolon-chained duty of care, then a hard register shift into legal boilerplate**

> Our greatest hope is that Project Dissident can herald a new era of alternative, community-driven, revolutionary theatre; that *Dissident* can transcend the business of theatre, and become an organizing tool and platform to spark popular political movements.
>
> That being said, we realize that voicing dissent, standing up against oppressive power, and taking revolutionary action comes with unique risks and perils for every artist and activist that vary from state to state and unique international and local context. In some places, free speech and artistic expression may be a well-protected constitutional right; in other places, producing anti-authauritarian thought, art, and organizing platforms may be met with with unjust surveillance, detention, imprisonment, torture, and even death by the deplorable systems and governments they confront.
>
> We must include a cautionary note here all of dissident creators and collaborators: think thoughtfully and responsibly about the consequences of your actions; consider the impact that your production may have on the lives of associated artists, activists, and local community members; make plans and adaptations to protect the safety, dignity, and wellbeing of performers, crew, audiences, and surrounding communities – especially those most vulnerable to retaliation, and the innocent people that we seek to empower.
>
> Within the United States, we affirm unequivocally that *Dissident* is a fictional artistic work and that producing political theatre—including protest and dissent-oriented art—is a constitutionally protected act of free expression. To assert this right, we put forth the following:
>
> **Dissident is a work of fiction.**
> All characters, events, dialogue, and representations contained in this script and recorded album are fictional. Any resemblance to actual persons, living or dead, or to real events, governments, institutions, or political movements is purely coincidental and not intended as factual representation.
>
> **This work is a creative and expressive artistic work protected under the Constitution of the United States, including the First Amendment right to free speech.**
> Any themes of dissent, protest, revolution, resistance, violence, or critique of power are presented solely in a fictional, artistic, and expressive context.
>
> Nothing in *Dissident* is intended to advocate, instruct, incite, or promote real-world violence, criminal activity, or unlawful conduct. References to acts of revolution, civil unrest, or violent political struggle are allegorical and narrative in nature and should not be interpreted as literal threats, instructions, or calls to violent action.
>
> No portion of this work shall be used as a basis for censorship, legal retaliation, government reprisal, or punitive action against the authors, rights holders, or licensed producers for exercising constitutionally protected artistic expression.

## Reusable phrases

Signature vocabulary and constructions this author returns to:

- **Naming power plainly:** "systems of oppression", "international systems of empire", "state repression", "oppressive institutions and conditions", "corrupted government power", "the deplorable systems and governments they confront"
- **People named without euphemism:** "oppressed people that survive, despite the system, at the margins of society and power", "marginalized and often unheard voices", "immigrant, low-income, and oppressed people", "the uninitiated", "neglected community members"
- **Movement verbs:** "subvert, adapt, and change", "rally", "spark popular political movements", "build grassroots people power", "take organized action", "herald a new era"
- **Organizer's framing:** "grassroots community organizer", "movement-making", "mutual aid", "community listening sessions", "an anchor and community-building space", "an organizing tool and platform"
- **Permission and flexibility language:** "Adaptable by design", "decentralized platform and vehicle", "carte blanche", "you are hereby granted permission to make it your own", "choose your own adventure" style, "ever-evolving vehicle for diverse voices"
- **Scrappiness as a value:** "be scrappy", "shoestring budget", "Scrappy Creatives", "prioritize content delivery over production value", "nontraditional and publicly-accessible performance spaces"
- **Hinges and qualifiers:** "That being said", "We only ask that", "We encourage you to", "How might you...", "in the spirit of", "Ultimately, we leave it up to you"
- **Paired-opposite constructions:** "light and dark, good and bad, conformist collaborator and rebellious conspirator", "In some places... in other places...", "the duality and contradiction of"
- **Humanist refrain:** "all human lives are worth fighting for and all human stories are worth telling", "all of our individual struggles are connected through our common humanity", "match your lived reality"
