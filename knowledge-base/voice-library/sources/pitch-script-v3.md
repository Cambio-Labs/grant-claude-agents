# Pitch Script v3 — voice reference

## What this source is

The working script for Cambio Labs' 5-minute investor/funder pitch, written to be spoken aloud. It runs four beats: what Cambio Labs is, one participant's story end-to-end, how the model scales, and the ask. The document also contains a meta-analysis of the pitch's narrative beats, open questions for Q&A, two alternative structural approaches, and a short AI policy statement.

Note: the doc also contains a full sample pitch for an unrelated product ("Upstart," by a different speaker) included only as a formatting example. That section is **not** Cambio Labs voice and is excluded from the excerpts below. What it does contribute is the script *format* — one idea per line, each line a landing point, so the speaker can connect and transition.

## How the voice works

- **Anaphora carries the structure.** "We co-create technology... We co-create courses... And finally, we co-create community spaces." The repetition is the argument — three things that only mean something multiplied together.
- **Rhetorical questions are the transitions.** Sections don't end, they hand off: "So what does this look like in practice?" / "So what does Tasha's entrepreneurial journey look like?" / "How might we scale?" The question is usually its own line.
- **One named person carries the whole middle.** Not a composite, not "our participants" — Tasha, with a FedEx job, a carrot cake business called "Eat my Cake Too," a teenage daughter, and a Harlem NYCHA apartment. The specificity is the point; the reveal that she's real lands at the end, not the start.
- **Friction stays in the story.** "She misses class sometimes because she is busy looking after her teenage daughter, but she still catches up asynchronously." The obstacle is named, not smoothed over — which is what makes the outcome credible.
- **Numbers arrive bare, without adjectives.** 1,000 learners. $600,000. 15,000. $1.7 million. $200,000. No "incredible," no "remarkable." The figure does the work.
- **The ask is specific and tiered**, not a lump sum: the headline number, then one spotlighted slice with exactly what it buys and for whom.
- **Vision is rendered as a place, not a projection.** "Imagine a future where..." followed by concrete scenes — phones, public libraries, city infrastructure, volunteer-run hubs — rather than market-size language.
- **Closes on a value, not a metric.** The last line reaches for belief and invitation, and it is unembarrassed about the word "revolution."
- **Written in short standalone lines,** each a complete thought that could be its own beat. Paragraphs are for the story sections; bullets are for the argument sections.

## Characteristic excerpts

**Opening — plain self-introduction, no throat-clearing**

> Hi, my name is Sebastián Martín, and I am the founder of Cambio Labs.

**The anaphora that defines the org**

> At Cambio Labs we co-create, by centering the voices of learners in low income communities, including underserved teenagers in schools and adults living in public housing.
>
> We co-create educational technology, like our gamified Journey platform, which makes learning engaging and fun. On Journey, learners can engage with multimedia content, complete challenges, and earn Gems that they can cash in for rewards. Journey can also be licensed and customized by other course creators.
>
> We co-create courses, like our social entrepreneurship and technology programs that we've delivered to approximately 1,000 youth and adult learners in New York City to date.
>
> And finally, we co-create community spaces, called Cambio Spaces - hubs based in NYCHA community centers, designed to provide free access to open learning, civic organizing, and social services.
>
> Cambio Labs is these three things, together: tech, courses, and spaces - built to economically empower low income people.
>
> So what does this look like in practice?

**Introducing a real person with concrete, un-generalized detail**

> Meet Tasha. Tasha works as a Fedex driver by day, but her real passion is baking! At night she bakes the most delicious carrot cake, which she sells at Farmers' Markets on weekends. She started a company called "Eat my Cake Too," which has gained a small following through word of mouth… but Tasha dreams of expanding her business to the point where she can work on it full time. Tasha lives in Harlem, and is a resident of NYCHA or public housing.
>
> So what does Tasha's entrepreneurial journey look like?

**Walking the participant experience in present tense**

> Tasha learns about the "Startup NYCHA" program through an info session at her local community center. She applies online, and after a simple interview, is accepted into our 6-month accelerator program! She shows up to a Cambio Space in Chelsea on a Saturday afternoon, where she meets her cohort of fellow entrepreneurs.
>
> Tasha is onboarded onto our Journey platform, a gamified learning platform that she will use to access our interactive entrepreneurial curriculum. Tasha loves the colorful design of Journey, and especially likes answering prompts to earn Gems, which she can cash in for real rewards like hours of coaching from experts! She also likes being at the top of her class' leaderboard, and feels motivated to keep learning.

**Naming the friction instead of smoothing it**

> Over the following 6 months, Tasha shows up to our Cambio Space on a weekly basis. She misses class sometimes because she is busy looking after her teenage daughter, but she still catches up asynchronously using her Journey account at home.

**The payoff, then the reveal that she's real**

> Besides being motivated by Gems and rewards, Tasha is also motivated by the real gains she is making in her business! By applying the lessons she learns on Journey, in just a few months she has developed a new website, attended various markets, and has broken into the catering market which provides her with more consistent income. Tasha completes her final pitch, and wins third place and a $1,000 cash reward! Using her brand new pitch deck, Tasha applies for other accelerators: gaining access to commercial space at Mangrove Kitchen, and a $20,000 grant from NYC Boss Up! Tasha is now also applying to become a Startup NCYHA instructor for future cohorts.
>
> This is the real story of Tasha, who is now a proud member of our alumni community. My question tonight is: how can we replicate Tasha's story all over New York City, and the world?
>
> How might we scale?

**Traction stated flatly, including the unglamorous parts**

> Cambio Labs has grown over the past 5 years, reaching 1,000 learners to date. We are on track to generate $600,000 dollars this calendar year. Although we are a nonprofit, the majority of our income has come from earned revenue strategies - primarily through the sale of courses and Journey software licensing.
>
> This past year has marked many important milestones for us, including:
> - Partnering internationally with a national nonprofit in India
> - Securing our first NYC City Agency contract by being awarded the People's Money grant
> - And securing 3 sites at NYCHA community centers to launch Cambio Spaces as weekly community hubs in 2026.

**Admitting what the org learned the hard way**

> We will continue to activate Cambio Spaces in low income areas. We have realized that developing technology and courses is not enough - our work is about building community and the physical infrastructure around our programs too.

**The argument for technology, in one sentence**

> Technology must be at the forefront of our strategy for scale, because after initial development, software is virtually free and infinitely available across every corner of the planet.

**Vision as concrete scenes, not projections**

> Imagine a future where the Journey app is delivering programs to hundreds of thousands of learners on phones,
> In public libraries and embedded in city infrastructure,
> And in Cambio Spaces - open learning hubs in every city, run by volunteers

**The ask — headline, then a spotlighted slice**

> So here is what we need to scale.
>
> We are seeking $1.7 million to execute our roadmap next year. This funding would cover key investments into Journey, team expansion, and the rollout of three Cambio Spaces in New York City.
>
> I want to specifically focus on one key area of philanthropic investment tonight:
>
> We are seeking $200,000 to sponsor *free access* to Journey courses for 10,000 new learners and course creators globally. Locally in NYC, we want to offer Journey, for free, to our alumni social entrepreneurs, small nonprofits in need of technology support, and public libraries. Internationally, we want to partner with a variety of organizations that are already on the front lines serving the most marginalized populations, in developing countries, rural areas, and conflict zones.

**Framing the unknown as something the money buys**

> By offering Journey for free to thousands of new users, we hope to learn what kind of infrastructure we need for global scale, and how to overcome challenges in bringing education to millions of the most disconnected people, by leveraging enmeshed internet networks, solar powered learning stations, and offline learning strategies.

**Closing — asks for more than money, lands on belief**

> Besides funding, we are also looking for advisors, program partners, technologists, and volunteers that want to support our vision of delivering universal access to education and economic opportunity - now, through the power of technology.
>
> Join our revolution of empathy, and the simple belief that all humans deserve a chance at meaningful learning and economic opportunity.
>
> Thank you!

**Taking a position against the field (AI strategy section)**

> Cambio AI, ethical AI, built on open-source models, with a true nonprofit foundation, and independent of big tech. Built to deliver personalized, multi-lingual, differentiated learning to every underserved human on the planet. While big AI is mired in profiteering, misinformation, and environmental and societal harm, we intend to build AI for humans. AI for good. AI for access. This is not just a product vision. It is a moral technological stance, and we are ready to lead.

## Structural note — the script format

The author's own stated method, worth reusing for any spoken piece:

> I usually break each slide into a set of lines so it's easy for me to connect and transition. Each slide has a clear purpose if it doesn't have a line and/or clear takeaway I remove it.
