# Advisory Board Meeting, Feb 2026 — status + 2026 strategic vision

## What this source is

A ~12-minute address to Cambio Labs' advisory board — a room of people who already know and have
funded the organization. It runs the whole arc: founder origin story, a chapter-by-chapter history of
the org's five years, unvarnished financials including a real income decline, and a closing
futures-framing exercise ("probable, plausible, possible") that turns into an ask for the room's
thinking, not just their money. This is the single richest source in the library for how Cambio talks
to people who are already inside the tent — closer to a family update than a pitch.

## How the voice works

- **Opens by naming the relationship before the content.** "A lot of folks in this space have been
  working with Cambio for a long time... we really value your trust and your partnership." The warmth
  is specific to the room, not a generic thank-you — it names what this particular audience has
  actually given (time, funding, trust) before asking for more.
- **Structures a five-year history as numbered chapters, said aloud.** "Chapter One: Early Programs
  and Workforce Training... Chapter Two, Growth, Schools, and Journey... Chapter Three..." This is a
  distinct structural device from anything else in the library — it's how Cambio narrates its own past
  to insiders, and it makes a long spoken history trackable in real time without slides.
- **States financial numbers with the loss right next to the win, same paragraph, no separation.**
  "Our income really exploded in 2024. We got from about $200,000 to $420,000. But we also lost some
  income because the solar program... decided to kind of go their own route." Growth and loss share a
  sentence; nothing is held back for a "however" three paragraphs later.
- **Names an unmet need or a downturn as a plain fact, then immediately reframes it as decision-making
  material — never apology.** "The sad part of all this is that... our income has taken a hit in this
  last year... I do not want to sugarcoat this either. There are very real challenges and constraints
  that we're facing now, and that's part of the reason why we're tapping into our broader network."
  This is the audit's Rule 03 (name gaps plainly) in its purest form — spoken, to funders, about money,
  without softening.
- **The origin story is personal and admits uncertainty and failure in it.** "I was not certain about
  my future path. I never thought it would be business." Failed and half-formed early ventures (Cambio
  Co., selling handicrafts at 20 years old) are told as "first learnings," not cut for polish.
- **Uses a three-tier futures frame as a rhetorical structuring device, not just content.** "Probable,
  plausible, and possible futures... What's likely to happen? ... What's plausible given what we know?
  ... what is possible if we were to collectively pool our knowledge?" This scaffolds the entire
  closing ask — it's a reusable rhetorical shape for any "help us think about what's next" moment, not
  specific to this one talk.
- **Closes on a cascade of short, unresolved questions rather than a single ask.** "What is possible?
  What is the future we want for our families and children? And how might we co-create our future
  together, right now, in this space?" No statement follows to answer them — the questions are the
  ending.
- **Team credit is specific and named**, not generic gratitude: "thanks a lot to the work of Michelle,
  we onboarded tons of volunteers and interns." Real names, real functions, attached to a real result.

## Characteristic excerpts

**Opening — names the room's specific relationship before anything else**

> A lot of folks in this space have been working with Cambio for a long time. Some folks have given
> us funding as well, donations and grants. So we really value your trust and your partnership, and
> we're really excited to basically activate all of your minds and your networks at a very pivotal
> moment for us.

**Origin story — admits uncertainty as the starting point, not a footnote**

> I was not certain about my future path. I never thought it would be business. But eventually, as I
> grew up and became an adult, I discovered social entrepreneurship and the power of using business
> practices and innovation to create social change.

**Origin story — a real, small, unpolished first venture**

> I would go to Bolivia and buy all these handcrafts for very cheap from local artisans. I was
> studying in the UK at the time, and I would go there and sell them at local farmers markets and use
> the profits to support an orphanage in La Paz, Bolivia... So that was me, kind of 20 years old,
> trying to experiment a little bit. But those were my first learnings, I think, as a social
> entrepreneur.

**Chapter structure as a spoken narrative device**

> Chapter One: Early Programs and Workforce Training. Now I'm going to offer a very high-level
> synthesis of the past four and a half years. I think there have been different phases of Cambio
> Labs and different chapters, and I'll walk you through kind of what our personal journey has been
> like.

**Growth and loss in the same breath, no buffer between them**

> Our income really exploded in 2024. We got from about $200,000 to $420,000. But we also lost some
> income because the solar program that we were part of decided to kind of go their own route and not
> implement the tech as much... Also, in San Francisco, there was a new mayor that took over, and
> that kind of changed the guard, and we lost our mayoral contracts out there.

**Naming a downturn plainly, to funders, without dressing it up — Rule 03 in the wild**

> But the sad part of all this is that there are a lot of wins, but our income has taken a hit in
> this last year. Especially, we have had no school partnerships and youth partnerships besides our
> summer Prep for Prep program. The schools really retracted from the contracts... But I do not want
> to sugarcoat this either. There are very real challenges and constraints that we're facing now, and
> that's part of the reason why we're tapping into our broader network to convene around what's next
> for us.

**Naming the specific kind of funding needed, not just "funding"**

> We have a lot of funding actually committed this year to specific programs, whether that's a solar
> program, workforce program, a program in Harlem, Startup NYCHA. We have a lot of funders who have
> given us money for specific projects, but we really need to tap into unrestricted funds that can
> fund our team and our growth to really build the next phase of Cambio Labs.

**The what/why/how structure, stated explicitly as a device**

> Just to wrap up here, there's the what of what we do... But I think when we combine this with our
> why, I think this is really what drives us and what makes us innovative... And the how, I think, is
> also what we've kind of figured out a bit.

**The futures-framing device that structures the whole closing ask**

> Probable, plausible, and possible futures. There's the more practical: if nothing happens, where
> are we headed? What's likely to happen?... And we want to push you to think beyond that... What is
> plausible given what we know about the world around us? But more interestingly, what is possible if
> we were to collectively pool our knowledge and change our economic constraints?

**Closing — a cascade of open questions, no answer supplied**

> So, what is possible? What is the future we want for our families and children? And how might we
> co-create our future together, right now, in this space?
