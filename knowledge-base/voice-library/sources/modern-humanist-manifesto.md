# The Modern Humanist Manifesto — voice reference

## What this source is

A political manifesto — the opening "Call to Action" plus the first chapter, "Humanist Government" — written to launch a movement (humanistrevolution.org). Its argument runs in three beats: our governments have been corporatized and corrupted so that wealth and power concentrate in the 1%; the resulting human suffering is documented in cited global statistics and in named individual lives; therefore the only legitimate purpose of government is to secure the material welfare of every human, and the next revolution must be one of care rather than ideology. It is heavily footnoted (56 sources) but written for a general public audience, not an academic one. The intended reader is an ordinary working person who already senses the system is rigged and needs permission to call it what it is and a concrete thing to do about it.

## How the voice works

- **Long colon-hinged catalogue sentences, then a short verdict.** A paragraph typically opens with a flat declarative thesis, drops a colon, and then piles four to six crises together with "and… and… and, perhaps most worryingly," running 90–110 words before landing on a short corrective sentence. The rhythm is accumulation → snap. A generic nonprofit voice breaks that into three tidy sentences and loses the sense of a load being stacked on the reader.
- **Anaphora across stacked one-sentence paragraphs.** Repeated stems get their own paragraph with white space around them — "The next revolution will be one of…" four times, "why we… why we… why we…", "Every child… Every human life…" It reads like speech transcribed, not like prose composed.
- **The word is always "humans," never "people," "individuals," "communities," or "beneficiaries."** And humans are *counted*: "400 million unemployed or underemployed humans," "24,000 humans die from hunger," "4.5 billion humans." The noun choice is itself the argument, and it is used with total consistency — this is the single most portable fingerprint of the voice.
- **Named vignette in present tense, then a hard pivot to a bolded global statistic inside the same paragraph.** The formula is fixed: country, first name, age in parentheses, one physical detail (bleeding hands, an Algebra worksheet, a makeshift curtain in a car), then the number. Never the story alone, never the number alone. Vignettes are unresolved — nobody is rescued.
- **"We" is accusatory at least as often as it is aspirational.** "We have allowed for the total corporate capture of the congress…" / "That's why we walk past mentally ill and homeless people on the street corner." The author implicates himself and the reader before asking for anything. Institutional nonprofit "we" means the organization; here "we" means the species.
- **Anger is delivered as documentation, not as adjectives.** Instead of "outrageous" or "unacceptable," the voice supplies mechanisms and figures — Citizens United, Super PACs, tax havens, an 8.2% effective rate — and only then names the thing in five words: "This is corruption, plain and simple!" Exclamation points are rationed to roughly one verdict per section.
- **Punctuation habits: trailing ellipses, aggressive bold, en-dash interruptions.** The "…" lets a list sag under its own weight mid-sentence rather than closing it. Bold marks the thesis sentence of nearly every paragraph, so the document is legible at two speeds — skim the bold and you have the manifesto. Dashes interrupt to insert a definition or an aside without a footnote.
- **It preempts the "radical" charge instead of escalating past it.** "This is far from a fringe or radical notion" / "This is not a radical idea, but a reclaiming of the power of everyday people." The rhetoric is maximalist; the self-positioning is deliberately reasonable.
- **Hope arrives late and in the imperative mood.** After the bleakest passage in the document, the turn is a stack of verbs the reader can act on today: "Organize, volunteer, start a local community space, start voting blocks, run for political office." No forward-looking abstraction, no "imagine a world where."

## Characteristic excerpts

**Opening move — catalogue sentence into a short corrective**

> The twenty-first century has heralded unique and existential challenges, and our established political and economic systems have failed humanity. Although there have been many advances in the lot of humanity since the twentieth century - with literacy, poverty, and mortality rates generally better than one hundred years ago - our antiquated and corrupted systems are failing us now. In the wake of the supposed end of the Cold War, the triumph of hyper-capitalism has allowed for corporations and billionaire owners of capital to control the globe: imperial and colonial proxy wars are waged for the acquisition of natural resources and contracts, levels of poverty and income inequality are excessively and unnecessarily high, technology has advanced rapidly, beyond our wildest imaginations, to the point of mass automation and human obsoletion, and perhaps most worryingly, we are on the brink of ensuring our own climate annihilation and erasure as a human species due to the unfettered proliferation of fossil fuels and pollutant industries. We need nothing short of a social, political, and economic revolution to course correct if humanity has any hope of survival, and for future generations to have an iota of the human dignity, safety, and opportunity that has been afforded to us by past generations.

**Signature cadence — anaphora across stacked paragraphs**

> The next revolution will be one of **care**, of the **heart**, and centered on the simple belief that **all humans deserve a chance of opportunity, justice, love, and happiness.**
>
> The next revolution will be one of **love**, for our **families and children**. Of **empathy** for our neighbors and people that we've never met.
>
> The next revolution will be rooted in caring for our most downtrodden and oppressed humans on the planet. In **recognizing the common humanity of all people**. In organizing as local and global citizens in a spirit of cooperation to save our shared neighborhoods, our common planet, and our collective human destiny.
>
> **The next revolution will be deeply** **Humanist**, benefiting all people and enshrining the rights and liberties of each human being (current and future) on this planet. We need bold Humanist leadership to meet the moment and **avert impending climate disaster and human extinction.**
>
> **The human revolution must be now.** Let us demonstrate our capacity for love and goodness and intelligence as a species that can outlive its own greed and barbarity. Let us all have a common faith in humanity, and in our common ability to care and hope and build community.

**Call to action — verbs the reader can do today, then a two-sentence sign-off**

> **This is a call to action.** Let this be a rallying cry, a modern solution, a movement for present and future humans. Join us, visit: humanistrevolution.org. Organize, volunteer, start a local community space, start voting blocks, run for political office, **build local human power.**
>
> Humanity deserves more. Let's take our world back.
>
> **Join the Humanist Revolution**

**Defining the premise — a claim, then the same claim narrowed to three words**

> We launch our movement on a simple and overarching premise: **the purpose of government is to ensure the welfare of its humans. All of its humans.** In all decision making around the intervention of the state, or spending from the public sector, maximizing material benefits for the most humans should take precedence over anything else.

**Naming an adversary — a list of interests, an ellipsis, a bolded verdict, then immediate data**

> **Our current economic and political systems are not serving most humans, or everyday working people and families.** Our current governments have been thoroughly corporatized and corrupted to prioritize other interests, like national security enforced by a military-industrial complex, conquest of global natural resources, wealth for politicians and powerbrokers, and total impunity for corporations and the rich… The majority of people - made up of everyday working humans - have been shortchanged and economically exploited by the powers that be. **We have all been failed by our corporatized and privatized governments.**
>
> This is far from a fringe or radical notion: it is evident in every feature of modern life, and most people in the world would agree. In 2025, in our modern era of late stage capitalism, the richest 10% owns over 75% of the world's total wealth. In contrast, the poorest half of the global population holds less than 2% of all wealth.

**The blunt interjection — five words after two pages of citations**

> **This is corruption, plain and simple!** And this isn't just an American problem. Across the world, in South Asia, Africa, and Latin America, governments have been bought and captured by the elite and corporations that influence laws so that they can benefit from tax cuts, deregulation, privatization of public industries, unfair government subsidies and contracts, and the rights to exploit lands, waters, and natural resources –all at the expense of global climate mitigation efforts, public trust and democratic systems, and vital social services for the safety and wellbeing of the majority of humans.

**Vignette-to-statistic pivot — the core structural move, twice**

> In Bangladesh, Rahima's (9) scarred hands are bleeding again during her daily 12-hour shift, as she sews clothes in a poorly ventilated sweatshop that supplies a global fast fashion brand. She is illiterate and earns well below the minimum wage to provide a meager income stream for her parents and siblings. **Nearly 138 million children work instead of attending school, 40% of them in hazardous industries.**
>
> In Afghanistan, Asma (15) is completing an Algebra worksheet when a bomb goes off in the courtyard outside of her classroom. She wakes up in the hospital and is informed that 85 of her schoolmates were killed in the blast, that the Taliban have regained control of the government, and that all girls are banned from attending school. Asma's parents force her to marry, selling her off to an abusive older man. She is now out-of-school, laboring for her in-laws as a domestic servant in their house; pregnant, depressed, and devastated to learn that she'll soon be having a daughter. **A total of 272 million children and youth (nearly 1 in 6 globally) are "out-of-school", mostly due to poverty, gender discrimination, and war** - with the highest numbers in Sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and conflict zones.

**Why statistics fail — the most distinctive prose in the document**

> These are real stories about real humans living on our planet – each one representing just a tiny fragment of the unfathomable scale of pain and suffering that is constantly swirling around us. When we read or hear statistics about billions or millions of people affected by a crisis, the size and scope is too much for our brains to process… it's not possible for us to comprehend or empathize at that scale: imagining entire stadiums, or city blocks, or oceans of individual bodies and souls, mothers and fathers and sons and daughters, with hopes and dreams of the future – all stacked up on top of each other, lamenting and screaming and crying and starving and dying in horrific, cacophonous unison. It seems otherworldly and hellish, but it is very real, and is happening all over the planet, right now, and all of the time. If we were to truly grasp and absorb all of that human suffering and pain, it would break our psyches and spirits. That is why when we hear about the mass execution and rape of thousands of innocent civilians in Sudan, or witness the indiscriminate bombing of tens of thousands of mothers and children in Gaza, or realize that in the next few decades extreme heat waves and superstorms will cause the painful deaths and drownings of hundreds of thousands of humans, yearly, across various regions of our ever-warming Earth– we must abstract, compartmentalize, rationalize, or ignore, for the sake of our own sense of sanity, humanity, and irrational hope for the future. This is how we must cope, and make meaning of our lives and pursuits of happiness in what can often feel like a dark and terrible world.

**Implicating the reader — then a three-word paragraph as the landing**

> This is why most humans work to produce the best that they can for themselves and their immediate families before they die, and do not have the luxury or bandwidth to engage in community aid, collective power building, or intergenerational movement-making. That's why we walk past mentally ill and homeless people on the street corner, and do not reach into our pockets to offer some change; why we take intercontinental flights to explore the world and enjoy vacations, even though our resulting personal carbon footprint exceeds a ton in burnt jet fuel – more than most people on the planet emit in a year; why we vote for politicians and parties that reinforce special treatment for corporations and the wealthy, at the expense of our neighbors' welfare… because we can't solve all of the world's problems; we must be practical, and rationally self-interested, and choose our battles. And in the final calculus, we must accumulate wealth or face poverty, and to some degree, we must all accept, conform, and submit to our governmental and societal oppression, because we are "only human," and we must each survive as best as we can.
>
> **Humans deserve more.**

**The proclamation, then the disarming close**

> **We proclaim:** the sole and foundational purpose of organized government is to serve human quality of life, and to ensure its citizens basic human needs and dignities: civil and civic rights, safety, and inalienable basic access to food, medical services, housing, education, and economic opportunity. **Every child deserves a chance at survival**, or of overcoming their genetic fortune or poverty within their lifetime. **Every human life is precious**, and must be protected, cared for, and provided the conditions to reach its full potential and capacity for meaning, purpose, and happiness.
>
> This is not a radical idea, but a reclaiming of the power of everyday people and a resetting of the true responsibility of government.
