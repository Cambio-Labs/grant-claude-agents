# Spoken / civic voice

## When to use this

Speeches and remarks, donor emails and thank-yous, advisor and board updates, testimony, institutional
partner letters, event copy. Writing addressed to someone who already knows Cambio Labs and has
standing — a supporter, a board member, a public body.

**Current backing covers the board/strategy register well** (status updates, candid financials,
major-gift-adjacent asks to an informed insider audience). It does **not** yet cover the ceremonial
register (an acceptance speech's warmth and gratitude) or institutional testimony (addressing a public
body like a City Council commission) — those need `hudson-guild-speech.md` and
`cec-commission-presentation.md`, both still awaiting transcript. If the piece is a speech or
testimony rather than a board/donor update, treat this card's traits as a starting point only and say
so in the output.

## What it sounds like

Warm toward the specific room, not warm in general. Candid about numbers, including bad ones, in the
same breath as good ones. Personal history is told with its uncertainty and early failures intact,
not smoothed into a clean success narrative.

## Signature moves

1. **Name the relationship before the content.** Open by naming what *this specific audience* has
   actually given — time, funding, trust — not a generic thank-you. "A lot of folks in this space have
   been working with Cambio for a long time... we really value your trust and your partnership."
2. **State a loss in the same sentence or paragraph as a win, no buffer between them.** "Our income
   really exploded in 2024. We got from about $200,000 to $420,000. But we also lost some income
   because..." Never a "however" three paragraphs later — the qualifier sits right next to the claim.
3. **Name a downturn as a plain fact, then reframe it as material for the room to act on — never as an
   apology.** "I do not want to sugarcoat this either. There are very real challenges and constraints
   that we're facing now, and that's part of the reason why we're tapping into our broader network."
4. **Tell the origin story with its uncertainty intact.** "I was not certain about my future path. I
   never thought it would be business." Failed or half-formed early ventures are kept in as "first
   learnings," not cut for polish.
5. **Structure a long update as explicit, named chapters, said aloud.** "Chapter One... Chapter Two...
   Chapter Three..." — makes a long spoken history trackable without slides. Useful for any long
   status update, not just this one.
6. **Name the specific kind of support needed, not support in general.** "We really need to tap into
   unrestricted funds that can fund our team and our growth" — not "we need your support."
7. **Credit people by name and function**, not generic gratitude: "thanks a lot to the work of
   Michelle, we onboarded tons of volunteers and interns."
8. **Close on open questions, not a resolving statement.** "What is possible? What is the future we
   want for our families and children? And how might we co-create our future together, right now, in
   this space?" No answer follows — the cascade of questions is the ending.

## Lines that exemplify it

> A lot of folks in this space have been working with Cambio for a long time. Some folks have given
> us funding as well, donations and grants. So we really value your trust and your partnership.

> Our income really exploded in 2024. We got from about $200,000 to $420,000. But we also lost some
> income because the solar program that we were part of decided to kind of go their own route.

> But the sad part of all this is that there are a lot of wins, but our income has taken a hit in
> this last year... I do not want to sugarcoat this either.

> So, what is possible? What is the future we want for our families and children? And how might we
> co-create our future together, right now, in this space?

## What to avoid

- A win-only update. If there's a loss, it sits next to the win, not omitted or buried past it.
- Softening a downturn into momentum language — this is the exact failure mode Rule 03 (in
  `../audit/voice-audit-guide.md`) names, and this source is direct evidence of the alternative.
- A polished, uncertainty-free origin story. The early failures are part of the credibility, not a
  liability to edit out.
- Using this card's traits for a *ceremonial* speech (an acceptance, a thank-you to a room) or for
  institutional testimony — see the scope note above. Those need different sources.
