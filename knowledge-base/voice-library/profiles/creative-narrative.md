# Creative / narrative voice

## When to use this

Social posts, campaign copy, newsletter openers, founder narrative, storytelling that needs to land
in a few lines. Use it when the reader is scrolling or skimming and the writing has to earn attention
on rhythm rather than on information.

## What it sounds like

Fast, stacked, and unhedged. It shifts register without apologizing — manifesto to logistics to
legal boilerplate — and trusts the reader to keep up. Underneath the poetry there is always an
organizer doing arithmetic.

## Signature moves

1. **Let lists of three to six do the heavy lifting,** escalating concrete → abstract or personal →
   geopolitical: "street violence, grassroots activism, state repression, and international systems
   of empire." Generic prose picks one safe abstraction; this voice stacks the range and lets
   accumulation create the weight.
2. **Land paragraphs on paired opposites.** "light and dark, good and bad, conformist collaborator
   and rebellious conspirator." The antithesis is structural, not decorative.
3. **Address the reader in second person, as a co-conspirator, and give them authority outright.**
   "you are hereby granted permission to make it your own." "Honor the talents, stories, and causes
   of your local community."
4. **Follow every abstraction with operational specifics in parentheses.** "be scrappy (i.e., start
   with public readings, low-cost basic staging, and prioritize content delivery over production
   value)." The visionary register never floats free of logistics — the parenthetical is where the
   organizer shows up. This is the most transferable move in the whole library.
5. **Run rhetorical questions as cascades** — one sentence, many items, ending in a question mark.
   A brainstorm handed to the reader, not a query the writer answers.
6. **Concede, then hold the line.** "That being said," and "we realize that" precede the counter-
   pressure; the sentence after the concession is the harder ask, never a retreat.
7. **Vary sentence length hard.** Longest and most semicolon-chained exactly where the stakes are
   highest, then snap to five words: "*Dissident* is a work of fiction."
8. **Name roles by epithet-appositive.** Name, comma, function: "*CHASKA,* community leader and
   maternal figure, spearheading an underground resistance movement." The title tells you the moral
   position; no backstory, no hedging adjectives.
9. **Use "we," not "I,"** even for solo-authored work.

## Lines that exemplify it

> *Dissident* explores the duality and contradiction of light and dark, good and bad, conformist collaborator and rebellious conspirator within every human and every humanist revolution.

> Adaptable by design, the spirit of Project Dissident is to be a decentralized platform and vehicle for the voices, needs, struggles, narratives, and revolutionary causes of other artists and communities across the world.

> Take on the mindset of a grassroots community organizer.

## What to avoid

- Explaining the metaphor after using it.
- Even sentence lengths. The rhythm is the content here.
- Softening the political register into "systemic barriers" or "underserved communities" *when the
  subject is actually a system.* This voice names things.
- **Naming Cambio's own partners as the adversary.** This is the sharpest failure mode of this card.
  The source is a political work whose antagonists are capitalism, imperialism, and state power —
  and "state power" in the abstract is one thing, but NYCHA, the city agencies, and the school
  districts Cambio works with are partners, and for many participants they are also landlords and
  schools. A recruitment post that borrows this card's moves literally can read as anti-NYCHA and
  damage a live partnership. Borrow the structure and the moral clarity; drop the adversary framing.
  Check `../../org-profile.md` for who counts as a partner before naming anyone as a problem.
- Borrowing its intensity for funder-facing work. For proposals and LOIs, use `values-manifesto.md`
  under the constraints in `../../voice-profile.md` instead.

## Scope caution — long-form vs. short-form

The backing source is long-form front matter. This card is reliable for **structure and
direct address**; it is *not* a reference for a caption or a short post, where the row's genuinely
short source (`../sources/sparky-ai-tech-vision.md`) is still awaiting transcript. When writing
short-form social, borrow structure from here but report the register as extrapolated.

## Source caveat

The indexed source is the front matter to *Dissident* — logline, adapter's note, values, production
guidance, legal disclaimer — not the script, lyrics, or stage directions, which are not present in
the document. This card therefore describes the **manifesto-and-direct-address** register well and
says nothing reliable about the author's scene-writing or lyric voice. If narrative-scene reference
is ever needed, a new source has to be indexed.
