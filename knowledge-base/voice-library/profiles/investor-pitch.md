# Investor / pitch voice

## When to use this

Investor and accelerator pitches, funder presentations, partner decks, product vision copy — anything
where the reader or listener is deciding whether to back Cambio Labs, and where the goal is momentum
rather than compliance.

## What it sounds like

Confident without being slick. It moves fast, in short standalone lines, each one a complete thought.
It earns the ask by spending most of its length on one real person rather than on the organization.

## Signature moves

1. **Open plainly.** Name and role, no throat-clearing, no hook-first cleverness: "Hi, my name is
   Sebastián Martín, and I am the founder of Cambio Labs."
2. **Use anaphora to carry structure.** "We co-create technology… We co-create courses… And finally,
   we co-create community spaces." The repetition *is* the argument — three things that only mean
   something multiplied together.
3. **Transition with a question, on its own line.** "So what does this look like in practice?" /
   "How might we scale?" Sections hand off rather than end.
4. **Let one named person carry the middle.** Not "our participants" — Tasha, with a FedEx job, a
   carrot cake business called "Eat my Cake Too," a teenage daughter, a Harlem NYCHA apartment.
   Walk her whole arc in present tense. Reveal that she is real at the *end*, not the start.
5. **Keep the friction in.** "She misses class sometimes because she is busy looking after her
   teenage daughter, but she still catches up asynchronously." The named obstacle is what makes the
   outcome credible.
6. **State numbers bare.** 1,000 learners. $600,000. $1.7 million. No "incredible," no "remarkable."
   The figure does the work; an adjective in front of it does the opposite.
7. **Tier the ask.** Headline number, then one spotlighted slice with exactly what it buys and for
   whom. Never a single lump sum.
8. **Render the vision as places, not projections.** Phones, public libraries, city infrastructure,
   volunteer-run hubs — not market size or TAM language.
9. **Close on a belief, and ask for more than money.** Advisors, partners, technologists, volunteers
   — then land on the value.
10. **When speaking live, make structure explicit and gloss acronyms the page didn't need to.**
    Confirmed by comparing the written script to its actual delivery (`anew-final-pitch.md`): spoken
    delivery adds "Growth Strategy 1/2/3" labels the page left as paragraph breaks, and defines NYCHA
    inline ("New York City's public housing system") where the script assumed the reader already knew
    it. Adapting written Cambio copy for a live read should do the same — number the structure aloud,
    define what a live room might not already know.
11. **In a very short pitch (under ~3 minutes), state the problem in bare paired statistics before
    naming the product** — confirmed by `sparky-ai-tech-vision.md`. Two stats, back to back, no
    adjectives, covering both sides of who's affected (e.g., students *and* teachers, not just the
    beneficiary), then the product name.

## Lines that exemplify it

> Cambio Labs is these three things, together: tech, courses, and spaces - built to economically empower low income people.

> This is the real story of Tasha, who is now a proud member of our alumni community. My question tonight is: how can we replicate Tasha's story all over New York City, and the world?

> Technology must be at the forefront of our strategy for scale, because after initial development, software is virtually free and infinitely available across every corner of the planet.

> Join our revolution of empathy, and the simple belief that all humans deserve a chance at meaningful learning and economic opportunity.

> While big AI is mired in profiteering, misinformation, and environmental and societal harm, we intend to build AI for humans. AI for good. AI for access. This is not just a product vision. It is a moral technological stance, and we are ready to lead.

## What to avoid

- Leading with the organization's history instead of a person.
- Hedged asks ("we would be grateful for any support").
- Adjectives propping up numbers.
- Startup-deck vocabulary — TAM, disruption, unicorn, 10x. This voice takes a moral position on
  technology; it doesn't borrow the language of the people it's positioning against.
- Ending on logistics. The last line is always a belief and an invitation.
